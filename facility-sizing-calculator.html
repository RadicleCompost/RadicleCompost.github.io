Real facility design requires additional space and infrastructure beyond active composting pads. Depending on your site and throughput, you will also need to plan for:

  • Receival and pre-processing area — waste inspection, weighbridge, receival pad
  • Decontamination — removal of plastics, contaminants, foreign objects (FOs)
  • Shredding and size reduction — tub grinder, trommel, or chipper operation
  • Mechanical handling — loader, turner, or in-vessel equipment turning circles and traffic lanes
  • Curing area — material typically requires a further 4–12 weeks of curing post-active phase
  • Screening — finished compost screening to specification (usually 10–25mm)
  • Finished product stockpile — storage awaiting sale, testing, or despatch
  • Buffer and setback distances — from property boundaries, residences, and waterways
  • Leachate and stormwater management — bunded pads, drains, holding ponds
  • Site access, parking, and amenities

Facility sizing is highly site-specific. Soil type, topography, local planning requirements, feedstock composition, intended end market, and regulatory conditions all affect the design. These figures are a planning conversation starter — not a permit or consent document.
